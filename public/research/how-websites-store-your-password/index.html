How long would it take to steal your password?

I stored the same 20 passwords the four ways websites do and attacked them from a laptop. How long a password holds depends on two things: the password, and how the website stores it. The person signing up does not get to choose the second.

/ 01 · The four methods

What each way of storing a password is

A website should never keep your password as is. It keeps a hash: a digest produced by running the password through a formula, which cannot be reversed. When you log in, the site hashes what you type and compares it with the stored one. All four methods in the test do that, but they were built for different jobs.

MethodWhat it isWhat it was built forFit for passwords?
MD5A 128-bit digest from 1992. Very fast to compute.Checking that a file has not changed, like a fingerprint. It is broken even for that today.No. It is fast on purpose.
SHA-256A 256-bit digest from the SHA-2 family (2001). Fast, and sound as a fingerprint.Digital signatures, TLS certificates, file integrity.Not on its own. It is fast on purpose too.
bcryptA 1999 formula made for passwords. It is salted and has a cost: each step of cost doubles the work. Cost 12 means 4,096 repetitions.Storing passwords.Yes. OWASP asks for cost 10 at least.
Argon2idThe winner of the 2015 Password Hashing Competition. Salted, and it demands memory on top of time for every guess.Storing passwords, including against graphics cards.Yes. It is OWASP's first choice.

A salt is a random value, different for each user, mixed in with the password. Two people with the same password end up with different hashes. In the test, MD5 and SHA-256 were unsalted, as on many older sites.

/ 02 · Speed

One attack, four speeds

Whoever steals the database does not see the passwords, only the hashes. They have to guess, hash each guess and compare. How many guesses a second they get depends only on the method the website picked. These are the top speeds measured on the same laptop.

Why the speeds come out like this

  • MD5 and SHA-256 are short sums. A graphics card has thousands of small cores doing the same operation at once, and these formulas barely touch memory. That is how the RTX 4060 manages billions a second. SHA-256 runs about 3.9 times slower because its sum is longer.
  • bcrypt is expensive on purpose. At cost 12 it repeats its setup 4,096 times and, all the way through, keeps reading and writing a 4 KB table. Graphics cards handle that kind of memory access badly: many of their cores sit waiting.
  • Argon2id demands memory. Every guess needs 46 MiB. The laptop RTX 4060 has 8 GB, so at most about 170 guesses fit at once (a calculation). But with a single pass, each guess walks that memory once, and moving memory is exactly what a graphics card does fast.

Speeds measured during the attack, not taken from the tool benchmark. For MD5 and SHA-256 the top speed comes from the brute-force phase: in the dictionary phase the limit was reading the list from disk, about 500 million guesses a second. For bcrypt and Argon2id the hash itself is the limit in every phase. A machine with more or better cards would be faster; this test does not measure by how much.

With bcrypt and Argon2id the dictionary did not even finish within the 10 minutes per phase. At 317 guesses a second, going through rockyou and its variations would take up to about 35 days; at 2,478, about 4.

Three things I did not expect

  • ZoroGotLostAg@in!404 held, even with MD5. It has capitals, symbols and numbers. What sinks a password is being built from pieces everyone uses, which is what happened to CrazyCatLady.
  • PleaseUseMe looked common and held. It was not in rockyou, and the dictionary variations never reached it.
  • PorqueFi held, and it is still weak. It has 8 characters, letters only. The test brute force reached 6 or 7 characters; trying letters only, MD5 would find it in about 1.3 hours (a calculation). Surviving the test window does not make it strong.

/ 05 · Parameters

Why did the newer Argon2id hold for less time?

With the test settings, bcrypt held about 8 times longer: it was attacked at 317 guesses a second, against 2,478 for Argon2id. The reason is the configuration. Argon2id ran at the minimum OWASP recommends (46 MiB and one pass, the first option on its list). bcrypt ran above its own: cost 12, where the minimum is 10. What the test compared is two configurations. Argon2id with more memory or more passes would be slower to attack, and bcrypt at cost 10 about 4 times faster (a calculation: each step of cost doubles the work). Raising the parameters has a price: the server takes longer on every login.

/ 06 · What to do

How to protect yourself

  • A different password for every site. If one site stores it with MD5 and gets breached, that password falls in seconds. If you reuse it, every account using it goes with it.
  • A password manager to keep them. It lets you have a different random password per site without remembering any of them.
  • For the ones you memorise, a long phrase of your own, rather than something like P@ssw0rd!. Keep it away from pieces everyone uses.

If you build websites

Store passwords with bcrypt or Argon2id and check the parameters, because they set what each guess costs. The OWASP guide gives minimum values for each algorithm. Plain MD5 or SHA-256 is not fit for passwords.

/ 07 · Method

How it was measured, and its limits

  • 20 passwords are not a sample. They were picked by hand to test ideas; the results describe these 20, not passwords in general.
  • The same 20, stored all four ways with Python (hashlib, bcrypt and argon2-cffi): unsalted MD5 and SHA-256, bcrypt at cost 12 and Argon2id with 46 MiB, 1 pass and 1 lane.
  • A laptop with an RTX 4060 and hashcat 7.0.0. Speeds are the ones measured during the attack.
  • Three phases per storage method: a public leaked list with variations, combinations from a list of 354 word pieces (checked in code that none of the 20 was in it), and brute force for MD5 and SHA-256 only.
  • Time was capped: about 20 minutes of brute force for MD5 and SHA-256 (21 and 25 minutes in practice), 10 minutes per phase for bcrypt and Argon2id. Held means it did not fall within that time.
  • The 35 and 4 days are calculations, not measurements: how long the list with variations would take at the measured speed.
